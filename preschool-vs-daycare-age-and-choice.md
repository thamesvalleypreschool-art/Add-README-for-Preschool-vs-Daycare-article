## Which Is Better for a Child, Preschool or Daycare?

**Neither is better for every child.** Preschool is generally suitable for children aged around 3 years and above who benefit from a structured learning routine. Daycare is suitable for families who need longer hours of safe childcare, especially for younger children. The best choice depends on your child's age, your work schedule and the quality of the centre.

A well-managed daycare can be a better choice than a poorly managed preschool, and the opposite is also true. If your family needs both early learning and extended care, consider a centre that offers both services.

Before making a decision, check these important factors:

- **Safety:** Clean, secure and child-friendly premises.
- **Staff:** Trained, caring and experienced teachers or caregivers.
- **Daily Routine:** A balanced schedule for learning, meals, play and rest.
- **Communication:** Regular and honest updates about your child's activities and progress.

## What Age Is Best for Daycare?

**There is no single best age to start daycare.** Many centres accept children from a few months old to school age. The right time depends on your family's childcare needs and whether you have found a safe and caring environment.

Age requirements vary between centres. Some accept infants, while others start enrolment at around 1.5 or 2 years of age.

Younger children generally need more individual attention, suitable caregiver-to-child ratios and flexible feeding and sleeping routines.

Before enrolling your child, ask the daycare:

- What is the minimum admission age?
- How many children does each caregiver look after?
- Are separate arrangements available for infants and toddlers?
- How are feeding, sleeping and hygiene routines managed?
- How does the centre help new children adjust?
